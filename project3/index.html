03 / BEHIND THE VISUALIZATION

What the data can—and can't—tell us

Why this view?

Life expectancy at birth is one window into population health. A choropleth reveals geographic patterns, while linked country trajectories show that a single snapshot can hide how much—or how little—a country has changed.

How to read it

The map uses a sequential color scale: lighter colors mean lower life expectancy and darker colors mean higher. Gray countries have no matched observation in this dataset. A constant scale across years makes changes comparable.

How values are calculated

The year control selects one of the 12 five-year observations, from 1952 through 2007. Country values are displayed without interpolation; the median and range use the countries included in the current continent filter. Regional bars show arithmetic means of country values.

Important limitations

Gapminder covers 142 countries and ends in 2007, so these are historical patterns, not current estimates. Continental means give each represented country equal weight. Geographic area does not represent population, and differences do not by themselves establish causation.

Development process

The checkpoint explored country trends, regional means, distributions, extremes, and a static world map. The final visualization brings these explorations together through linked geographic and temporal views.

Interaction design

The year slider updates the map and summary statistics. Selecting a continent filters the map and highlights the regional chart while automatically comparing countries with the highest, middle-ranked, and lowest life expectancy in 2007. These countries remain fixed as the year changes. Users can customize the comparison by clicking countries on the map.
